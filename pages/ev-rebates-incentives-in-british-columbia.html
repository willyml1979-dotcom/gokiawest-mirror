Federal Incentives: The iZEV Program

The federal government's Incentives for Zero-Emission Vehicles (iZEV) program offers point-of-sale rebates on eligible new battery-electric, plug-in hybrid, and hydrogen fuel cell vehicles purchased or leased in Canada. As of the most recent program guidelines, eligible buyers can receive up to $5,000 toward the purchase of a fully electric vehicle, and up to $2,500 for certain plug-in hybrid models, depending on the vehicle's base MSRP and electric range.

To qualify under the iZEV program, the vehicle must appear on Transport Canada's list of eligible models, and the purchase price must fall below the program's MSRP cap — currently set at $55,000 for base trims, with higher-trim allowances up to $65,000 for certain vehicle categories. Several Kia models, including the EV6 and Kia Niro EV, have qualified under this program. Eligibility is applied at the point of sale, meaning the rebate is typically deducted directly from your purchase price rather than claimed later on a tax return.

It is important to note that the iZEV program is subject to available federal funding, and vehicles must be purchased or leased for personal or business use by a Canadian resident. Provincial Incentives: BC's CleanBC Go Electric Program

British Columbia offers one of the most generous provincial EV incentive programs in the country through the CleanBC Go Electric Passenger Vehicle Rebate. Administered by the Province of BC, this program provides rebates of up to $4,000 on eligible new battery-electric vehicles and up to $2,000 on eligible plug-in hybrid electric vehicles, depending on household income and vehicle pricing criteria.

Under the CleanBC program, there is also an additional rebate tier for lower- and middle-income households. Buyers whose household income falls at or below a qualifying threshold may be eligible for an enhanced rebate of up to $4,000 on top of the standard rebate — bringing the total provincial rebate to as much as $8,000 for qualifying buyers. This income-based component is administered separately and requires an application through the Province of BC's rebate portal.

When you combine the federal iZEV rebate with the provincial CleanBC rebate, eligible British Columbia residents can potentially access more than $9,000 in combined savings on a qualifying electric vehicle purchase. Vehicle price caps and eligible model lists for the CleanBC program are maintained by the Province and updated periodically, so we encourage buyers to review the current program details at cleanbc.gov.bc.ca.

Home Charging and Infrastructure Rebates

Owning an electric vehicle also means thinking about home charging, and there are incentive programs to help with that cost as well. The federal government offers rebates through Natural Resources Canada's Zero Emission Vehicle Infrastructure Program (ZEVIP) and, for eligible homeowners, rebates toward the purchase and installation of a Level 2 home charging station (EVSE). Depending on the program stream and your eligibility, rebates can cover a portion of equipment and installation costs.

In British Columbia, BC Hydro and FortisBC have historically offered rebates and incentives for residential EV charger installation. BC Hydro's EV charging rebate program has provided rebates of up to $350 toward the purchase of an eligible Level 2 home charger for residential customers. FortisBC has offered similar programs for customers in its service territory. These utility-based programs have their own eligibility requirements and funding cycles, so availability can vary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I combine the federal iZEV rebate and the BC CleanBC rebate on the same vehicle?

Yes, in most cases eligible buyers in British Columbia can stack the federal iZEV rebate and the provincial CleanBC Go Electric rebate on the same vehicle purchase, provided the vehicle qualifies under both programs. The combined savings can reach over $9,000 for qualifying buyers. Each program has its own eligibility criteria, including vehicle price caps and approved model lists, so it is important to confirm that your chosen vehicle and trim qualify under both programs before finalizing your purchase.

Is the federal EV rebate applied at the dealership or do I claim it on my taxes?

The federal iZEV rebate is applied as a point-of-sale discount, meaning it is deducted directly from the vehicle purchase price at the time of sale — you do not need to claim it on your income tax return. The rebate is processed through the dealership on your behalf. At Go Kia West, we handle the iZEV rebate application as part of the purchase transaction so the savings are reflected immediately in your final price.

Do plug-in hybrid vehicles qualify for EV incentives in BC?

Yes, plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs) can qualify for both federal and provincial incentives, though typically at a lower rebate amount than fully battery-electric vehicles. Under the federal iZEV program, eligible PHEVs with a minimum electric range may qualify for up to $2,500. Under the BC CleanBC program, eligible PHEVs may qualify for up to $2,000 in provincial rebates. Specific eligibility depends on the vehicle's electric range, MSRP, and whether it appears on each program's approved vehicle list.

Are there income requirements to receive EV rebates in British Columbia?

The standard federal iZEV rebate and the base CleanBC provincial rebate do not have income requirements — they are available to all eligible buyers regardless of household income, as long as the vehicle qualifies. However, BC's CleanBC program also includes an enhanced rebate tier specifically for lower- and middle-income households, which provides additional savings on top of the standard rebate. This enhanced portion does require an income-based application through the Province of BC's rebate system.

Do EV rebates apply to leased vehicles as well as purchases?

Yes, both the federal iZEV program and the BC CleanBC rebate program generally apply to eligible vehicles that are leased, not just purchased outright. For leased vehicles, there are typically minimum lease term requirements — for example, the iZEV program has historically required a minimum lease term of 48 months for the vehicle to qualify.
